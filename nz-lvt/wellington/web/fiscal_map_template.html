Net by suburb

Rates paid minus rates-funded cost of service, $ million a year. Suburbs with at least 50 rating units. Commercial areas pay far more than they cost because commercial property carries a 3.7× general-rate differential; large residential suburbs cost more than they pay.

How it's calculated

Rates

Each rating unit's 2025/26 bill from the rates resolution: general rate on capital value (commercial 3.7× residential), water, sewerage, stormwater, sector rates and the downtown levy. Modelled total $611.8m against the actual $627.7m.

Local network

709 km of council roads and about 2,700 km of council pipes from the council's GIS. Local streets and pipes up to 300 mm are charged to the properties along each side, by frontage. Bigger pipes cost more per metre.

Shared costs

Trunk mains, treatment, arterial roads and every other rates-funded service (parks, libraries, governance) are shared equally per rating unit; trunk stormwater by land area. Sharing by capital value instead narrows but doesn't close the commercial–residential gap.

Two cost views

Rates-funded spreads what rates pay for today, so the city nets to zero. Full cost adds unfunded depreciation (mostly three waters), $694m in total, about $82m more than rates raise.

Sources: Wellington City Council valuation roll (2024 revaluation), 2025/26 rates resolution, LTP 2024-34 Amendment activity statements, council GIS (roads, three-waters pipes), Stats NZ SA1 2025 and 2023 Census. Residential vs commercial status is estimated from zoning and property descriptions. A model, not the council's cost accounts.
