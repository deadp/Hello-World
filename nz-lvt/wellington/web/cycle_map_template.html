How it's worked out

Trips

2023 Census main means of travel to work and to education, from each SA2 of residence to each SA2 of workplace or school (Stats NZ). People who work or study at home are left out. Homes are placed by SA1 population, jobs by estimated floor-space employment, and education trips at schools and universities.

Routes

Every trip is routed over OpenStreetMap streets and paths bikes may use, with hills from the council's LiDAR elevation model: each metre climbed counts as ten on the flat. There's a mild preference for calmer streets, but routes mostly follow the most direct way, not today's safest one. Outbound and return trips are routed separately.

Who would cycle

The Propensity to Cycle Tool's Go Dutch model gives the chance a trip is cycled from its length and steepness, as if cycling were as normal as in the Netherlands. The e-bike version adds the PCT's e-bike terms, which remove most of the hill penalty. "Census 2023" shows the people who actually cycled.

Gaps

A street is a gap when it carries 250+ potential trips a day and is stressful to ride: over about 2,000 vehicles a day (council counts) or over 50 km/h, with no separated lane or path along it. Painted lanes don't count as protection on busy streets.

Limits

Only commuting and study trips are included, which is about a third of all trips. Shopping, leisure and school runs by parents aren't. OSM tagging and the 2022 plan layer may lag recent builds. The PCT model was fitted to English and Dutch travel, not Wellington's. Treat the rankings as a long list for engineers to test, not a design.

Sources: Stats NZ 2023 Census travel to work and education (SA2 origin–destination), SA1 population and 2024 business employee counts; OpenStreetMap contributors (streets, paths, schools; data May 2026); Wellington City Council 1 m LiDAR DEM (2020), road traffic counts and Strategic Bike Network 2022; uptake model from the Propensity to Cycle Tool (Lovelace et al. 2017).
