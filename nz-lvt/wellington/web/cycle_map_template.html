How it's worked out

Trips

2023 Census travel to work and to education from each SA2 to each SA2 (Stats NZ), with education split into primary, secondary and tertiary using the Ministry of Education school directory and rolls. Shopping, visiting and leisure trips are added the way Scotland's Network Planning Tool does it: trip rates relative to commuting, and a gravity model to OSM shops, parks, beaches and attractions and to other homes.

Streets, by direction

Every OpenStreetMap street and path bikes may use, rated separately in each direction: a protected uphill lane with sharrows downhill counts as protected one way only. Speed limits come from NZTA's National Speed Limit Register, traffic from council counts and NZTA state highway sites. Traffic stress (1–4) follows a speed × volume table (Furth, UK LTN 1/20, CROW, Auckland Transport): a 30 km/h street with under ~3,000 vehicles a day is fine to share. Busy junctions without signals or a marked crossing add crossing stress.

Routes, checked against counters

Trips are routed on the real streets with hills from the council's LiDAR. How much riders avoid busy streets, hills and unsignalised crossings is calibrated against the council's VivaCity sensor counts. Outbound and return trips are routed separately.

Who would cycle

Go Dutch uses the Propensity to Cycle Tool's current models (commute 2020; primary and secondary school models), never less than today's cycling. E-bike removes most of the hill penalty. "Wellington habits" uses a model fitted to Wellington's own 2018 and 2023 commuting, with every route made low-stress: a cautious lower bound. "Today" shows people who cycle now (census commuters, plus other trips at each area's commute cycling rate).

Gaps and crossings

A street is a gap where it carries 250+ potential trips a day and at least one direction is stressful with no protection. Dashed gaps are protected or calm one way only. Diamonds mark junctions where lots of potential trips arrive on quiet streets but must cross a busy road without signals.

Limits

Shopping, visiting and leisure trips are modelled from UK travel surveys, not measured in Wellington, and the counters can't confirm them. OSM tagging and the 2022 plan layer may lag recent builds, and counters mostly sit on routes that are already popular. The PCT model was fitted to English and Dutch travel, not Wellington's. Treat the rankings as a long list for engineers to test, not a design.

Sources: Stats NZ 2023 Census travel to work and education (SA2 origin–destination), SA1 population and 2024 business employee counts; OpenStreetMap contributors (streets, paths, schools; data May 2026); Wellington City Council 1 m LiDAR DEM (2020), road traffic counts and Strategic Bike Network 2022; NZTA National Speed Limit Register, state highway traffic sites; WCC transport sensors (VivaCity); uptake model from the Propensity to Cycle Tool (Lovelace et al. 2017; 2020 model).
